What drives the cost

When two quotes for "a web app" differ widely, it is usually because they describe different amounts of work. These are the factors that move the price most:

  • Scope and features: the number of screens, workflows and rules the software must handle.
  • Users and permissions: separate roles, such as customers, staff and administrators, each need their own views and access controls.
  • Integrations: connecting to payment providers, accounting tools, CRMs or existing systems adds design, build and testing work.
  • Data and reporting: migrating existing data and building dashboards or reports adds effort.
  • Design: a tailored interface takes more work than a standard one.
  • Hosting, security and support: where the software runs, how it is protected and who looks after it afterwards.

Ways to keep cost under control

  • Start with the one process that causes the most pain
  • Agree what is in and out of a first version
  • Settle the scope before development begins
  • Add features once the first version is working

Frequently asked questions

How much does custom software cost in South Africa?

There is no single price, because custom software ranges from a small internal tool to a large multi-user platform. The cost depends on what the software needs to do, how many types of users it has, how many other systems it connects to, and how much needs to be designed from scratch. A scoped proposal after a consultation is the reliable way to get a number for your project.

How much does a web app cost in South Africa?

A web application's cost follows the same drivers as any custom software: the number of features, the complexity of the workflows, user roles and permissions, integrations, and the level of design and testing involved. A simple tool with a few screens costs far less to build than a customer portal with payments, reporting and integrations.

Why won't you give a price before understanding the project?

Two projects that sound similar can differ a great deal in effort. Quoting without a scope tends to mean either padding the price for risk or under-quoting and changing it later. We would rather agree the scope first so the proposal reflects what you actually need.

Can I start small and add features later?

Often, yes. Many businesses start with the part of a process that causes the most pain, such as replacing a key spreadsheet, and extend the system once it is working. A consultation can help decide what belongs in a first version and what can wait.

What does a consultation involve?

It is a conversation about how your business works today, what is slowing it down, and what a good outcome would look like. The result is a clearer idea of whether custom software is the right move and what a first version would need to include.
